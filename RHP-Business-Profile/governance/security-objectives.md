# 🔐 RHP Security Objectives

## Purpose

This document defines the high-level information security objectives of **Raccoon Health & Pharmacy (RHP)**.

RHP is a fictional small independent retail, compounding, and specialty pharmacy used for cybersecurity and Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) home labs and portfolio projects.

These objectives establish the organization's security priorities and provide business context for future risk assessments, policy development, control assessments, compliance reviews, and other GRC activities.

---

# 🎯 Security Objectives

## 1. Protect Patient and Sensitive Information

RHP will protect sensitive information from unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, loss, or misuse.

Protected information may include:

- PHI and ePHI
- Prescription information
- Patient and contact information
- Insurance and claims information
- Payment information
- Employee information
- Authentication information
- Confidential business records

Security safeguards should be appropriate to the sensitivity and business importance of the information being protected.

---

## 2. Maintain Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability

RHP will support the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information and technology used in pharmacy operations.

**Confidentiality**  
Information should only be accessible to authorized individuals and systems.

**Integrity**  
Information and systems should be protected against unauthorized or improper modification.

**Availability**  
Systems and information necessary for pharmacy operations should remain available when needed or be recoverable within appropriate business requirements.

---

## 3. Control Access to RHP Systems and Information

Access to RHP systems and information should be limited according to business need and job responsibility.

RHP seeks to maintain:

- Unique user identification where supported
- Appropriate authentication
- Role-based or need-based access
- Privileged-access restrictions
- Account provisioning and deprovisioning
- Periodic access review where appropriate
- Multi-factor authentication where required or appropriate

Access should be removed or modified when it is no longer required.

---

## 4. Protect RHP Technology

RHP will implement reasonable safeguards to protect workstations, networks, applications, cloud services, pharmacy technology, and other information systems.

Security capabilities may include:

- Endpoint protection
- Firewalls and network filtering
- Secure system configuration
- Network segmentation
- Vulnerability and patch management
- Encryption
- Security logging and monitoring
- Malware protection
- Backup and recovery

Safeguards should reflect system criticality, information sensitivity, business requirements, and identified risk.

---

## 5. Support Secure Pharmacy Operations

Cybersecurity controls should support the safe and reliable operation of RHP's retail, compounding, specialty, and patient-service activities.

Security measures should consider the operational importance of:

- Prescription processing
- Prescription dispensing
- Pharmacy automation
- Compounding operations
- Environmental monitoring
- Patient services
- Payment processing
- Business administration

Security requirements should balance protection with RHP's need to maintain effective pharmacy operations.

---

## 6. Manage Cybersecurity Risk

RHP will identify, assess, document, and manage cybersecurity risks affecting its information, systems, services, vendors, and business operations.

Risk-management activities may include:

- Risk identification
- Likelihood and impact analysis
- Control evaluation
- Residual-risk analysis
- Risk treatment
- Risk ownership
- Remediation tracking
- Risk acceptance where appropriately authorized

Risk decisions should be documented and supported by available evidence.

---

## 7. Meet Applicable Requirements

RHP will identify and address applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, business, and internal security requirements.

Relevant sources may include:

- HIPAA Security Rule
- HIPAA Privacy Rule
- HITECH Act
- USP <797>
- USP <800>
- PCI DSS where applicable
- Applicable state requirements
- Contractual security and privacy requirements
- Internal RHP policies and standards

RHP may also use cybersecurity frameworks and guidance such as NIST CSF 2.0, NIST RMF, NIST Special Publications, CIS Controls, and ISO/IEC 27001/27002 to support its security and risk-management activities.

---

## 8. Manage Third-Party Risk

RHP will consider cybersecurity, privacy, compliance, operational, and business risks associated with third-party providers.

Third-party risk activities may include:

- Maintaining a vendor inventory
- Identifying vendor access to sensitive information or systems
- Reviewing relevant security documentation
- Evaluating contractual requirements
- Assessing critical vendor dependencies
- Tracking identified issues
- Periodically reassessing higher-risk relationships

---

## 9. Detect and Respond to Security Events

RHP will maintain capabilities appropriate to its size and environment for identifying, escalating, responding to, and documenting security events and incidents.

Relevant capabilities may include:

- Security logging
- Endpoint alerts
- Network-security alerts
- Identity and authentication logging
- Incident reporting
- Escalation procedures
- Incident response
- Breach evaluation where applicable

---

## 10. Support Resilience and Recovery

RHP will maintain reasonable capabilities to recover from technology failures, security incidents, data loss, and other disruptions affecting important pharmacy operations.

This may include:

- Data backups
- Off-site or cloud backup capabilities
- System recovery procedures
- Business continuity planning
- Disaster recovery planning
- Recovery testing
- Alternative or manual business processes where appropriate

---

## 11. Maintain Security Awareness and Accountability

RHP personnel should understand their responsibilities for protecting organizational systems and information.

RHP seeks to maintain:

- Security and privacy awareness
- Defined security responsibilities
- Appropriate workforce training
- Reporting mechanisms for suspected security events
- Accountability for compliance with organizational policies

---

## 12. Continuously Improve the GRC Program

RHP will periodically evaluate its cybersecurity and GRC program and address identified gaps.

Improvement activities may result from:

- Risk assessments
- Control assessments
- Audits
- Compliance reviews
- Security incidents
- Vendor assessments
- Vulnerability findings
- Policy reviews
- Changes to technology or business operations
- Changes to applicable requirements

Findings and remediation activities should be documented and tracked through completion or appropriate risk acceptance.
